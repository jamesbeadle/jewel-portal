### Question 6
Suspicious email or payment request -
You receive an email that appears to be from Nigel, Jeremy, a supplier or any other contact, asking you to make a payment, change bank details, buy gift cards, open an attachment or click a link. What should you do?
_(1 Point)_
_Single choice._
- Do it quickly if the email looks urgent
- Reply to the email and ask if it is genuine
- Stop and verify the request using a known, trusted phone number or separate contact method, and call Nigel or Jeremy if you are unsure
- Forward it to a colleague and assume they will deal with it

### Question 7

_(1 Point)_
_Single choice._
- Click the link and check whether the page looks genuine
- Use the link only if the logo and branding look right
- Forward your card details by email instead
- Do not click the link; verify the request separately and call Nigel or Jeremy if you are unsure

### Question 25

- Ask the venue staff for their Wi-Fi password and assume it's safe

### Question 27

_(1 Point)_
_Single choice._

- Forward the message to a colleague to deal with

### Question 28

_(1 Point)_
_Single choice._
- Plug it into your laptop to see what's on it and find the owner
- Do not plug it in. Hand it to IT/Jeremy so it can be safely checked or destroyed - unknown USBs can carry malware
- Take it home and check it on your personal PC
